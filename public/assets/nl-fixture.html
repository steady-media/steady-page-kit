Does playing 3D chess make your child aggressive?

A new study from the Institute of Recreational Cognition followed 400 families for a year. The results, published this week, complicate a decade of assumptions about strategy games and behaviour.

Researchers had braced for a link between competitive play and irritability. What they found pointed the other way, and the size of the effect surprised even the skeptics on the team.

“We expected the opposite. Children who played more reported feeling calmer, not angrier.”
Three-dimensional chess sets sold out in three countries last quarter.
  • The control group played two-dimensional checkers.
  • Self-reported mood was measured weekly.
